## r2 contract

- **Local DTO:** exact keys and an 8 KiB limit.
  - A per-key type and unset table.
  - Changes and unset are the explicit touched set, and `base.fields` is exactly that union.
  - **Epoch first**, then whole-patch refusal on any version mismatch for a touched field. Untouched fields rebase.
  - The origin enum is closed, checked for consistency and **carries no authority**. `requestId` is correlation only.
  - Absent, default and unset semantics, plus same-value and unset version bumps on a LOCAL commit. The MCP equal-value no-op stays read-only.
- **Committed snapshot:** one no-IO tuple (values, epoch, revision, per-field versions) that GET and internal reads share. There's no GET or display initializer.
- **Store turn:** publication happens once after `await replaceFile` resolves, outside the filesystem catch and the legacy rollback. `renameOver` and `replaceFile` are unchanged. The publication plan is data only, staged before the primary write.
  - A post-primary fault gives `committed_unconfirmed` plus degraded. Typed patches are then refused and emergency writes compose on the **actual base**.
  - Durability is process-level only, with no directory fsync and no power-loss claim.
  - Non-opted stores keep their optimistic cache, displaySnapshot timing, rollback, peek, audit and migration behaviour.
- **Renderer:** a committed-only cache, with drafts kept per field edit and request. Revision ordering applies within an epoch, an equal revision with different content is invalid, an epoch change invalidates, purge invalidates before any await, and nothing is automatically retried.
- **Callers:** every producer is migrated: Identity Alias and Color, the SettingsView saves, both palette toggles (reading committed state at invocation), and the getter consumers. No full-body fallback is allowed, and the search must be repeated on the implementation head.
- **Emergency and tool admission:** the original host, settings epoch, emergency generation and launch receipt are captured at entry, before the queue or Ask, and checked at every await and effect. Emergency increments the generation before the synchronous stop. MCP uses its original expected_state before the forward commit and the owned commit version afterwards. Restore applies only to the owned loopback field, and wasSuperseded means no rollback. Stale STOP, status and catch are fenced. The deadline doesn't release the queue during IO.
- **Recovery:** "Reload saved settings" is a privileged action.
  - Healthy means `not_degraded` with no IO.
  - It requires the main-sender check plus admission. It's queued, and the reread is strict and never uses the legacy loader.
  - A new random epoch with versions at 0. No runtime effects and no activation.
  - It uses a degradation id, and duplicates join or return the recorded result.

## B1 (required clarification): emergency persistence when the actual base is unknown

- §5 of the algorithm says an *uncertain primary outcome* marks the surface unavailable, pending a controlled reread.
- Step 8 and the recovery section cover emergency composition only when **post-primary publication** failed and the actual base is known.
- **Specify the emergency durable write when the actual primary base is unknown.** For example: the emergency turn performs the same strict controlled reread first, then composes network-off on that validated base. If the reread fails, durable emergency persistence is refused and reported, while the synchronous live-off stays in effect.
- It must never compose on the stale public tuple or on an unconfirmed candidate.
- Add a control: an uncertain primary outcome followed by an emergency disable, with a valid reread and then with a failed reread.

## L1 (liveness): the recovery invocation cap

- "At most 32 invocation records per degradation, with visible limit refusal without eviction" means 32 failed reload attempts on one degradation, for example while the file is transiently unreadable, **blocks recovery until restart**.
- **Fix:** apply the cap to **in-flight** records only, or let a completed failed record be retired once a newer attempt starts. Keep duplicate and lost-reply idempotence through the most recent N completed records.
- Add a control: more than 32 sequential failures followed by a successful reload.
